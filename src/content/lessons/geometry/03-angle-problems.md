## Why this matters

Surveyors, engineers and carpenters work out angles they cannot measure directly — the pitch
of a roof, the angle of a support strut — from the ones they can. Each step uses a known fact,
and writing down the **reason** is what turns a guess into a proof.

## The three shapes to look for

| Angle pair | Shape | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alternate | Z | equal |
| Corresponding | F | equal |
| Co-interior (allied) | C | add to $180^\circ$ |

These only work when the lines really are **parallel** — shown by matching arrows on the lines.

## Setting out a solution

Give each step on its own line with its reason in brackets:

> $\angle x = 70^\circ$ (corresponding angles)
> $\angle y = 180 - 70 = 110^\circ$ (angles on a straight line)

Examiners give marks for the reasons, not just the numbers.

## Kites and other shapes

Sahira thinks ABCD is a kite because AB = AD. A kite needs **two** pairs of equal adjacent sides.
To prove or disprove it, find the angles and see whether the shape is symmetrical about a
diagonal — one pair of equal sides is not enough.
