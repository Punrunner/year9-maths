  - title: 'Constructing 60° and 30°'
    problem: 'Construct an angle of $30^\circ$ at the point X on a line.'
    steps:
      - explain: 'With the compass point on X, draw an arc that cuts the line at A.'
      - explain: 'Without changing the width, put the point on A and draw an arc that cuts the first arc at Y.'

## Why this matters

Before protractors and computers, builders set out right angles for walls and foundations
with pegs and string using exactly these constructions. They are still the most accurate
way to draw angles by hand — a compass does not have the small reading errors of a
protractor.

## The toolkit

Every construction in this lesson is built from two moves:

1. **Equal arcs.** Two points the same distance from a third lie on a circle round it.
2. **Crossing arcs.** Where two arcs of equal radius from A and B cross, the point is the
   same distance from A as from B.

| Construction | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Equilateral-triangle arcs | $60^\circ$ |
| Perpendicular at a point on a line | $90^\circ$ |
| Perpendicular bisector of a line segment | $90^\circ$ through the midpoint |
| Perpendicular from a point to a line | $90^\circ$ from a point off the line |
| Angle bisector | half of any angle |

## Making other angles

Once you can make $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ and halve any angle, many more follow:

- $30^\circ$: bisect $60^\circ$. $\;15^\circ$: bisect $30^\circ$.
- $45^\circ$: bisect $90^\circ$. $\;22.5^\circ$: bisect $45^\circ$.
- $120^\circ$: two $60^\circ$ angles side by side, or $180^\circ - 60^\circ$ on a straight line.
- $135^\circ$: $90^\circ + 45^\circ$, or $180^\circ - 45^\circ$.

## Constructing a triangle from angles

To construct a triangle with a 6 cm base and base angles of $45^\circ$ and $30^\circ$: draw
the 6 cm base (measuring *this* is allowed — it is given), construct $45^\circ$ at one end
and $30^\circ$ at the other, and extend the arms until they meet.

> **Neat work matters.** Use a sharp pencil, make the arcs long enough to cross clearly, and
> never rub them out.
