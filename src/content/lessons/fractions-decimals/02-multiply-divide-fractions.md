## Why this matters

Recipes, scale drawings and measurements are full of fractions: *¾ of a 164½-litre tank*,
*a plank 4¼ m long cut into pieces of ⅝ m*. Multiplying and dividing them quickly and
without a calculator is a core Year 9 skill.

## Why dividing means multiplying by the reciprocal

You already know that $9 \div 3 = 9 \times \frac{1}{3}$ and $8 \div 2 = 8 \times \frac{1}{2}$.
Dividing by any number is multiplying by one over that number — its reciprocal.

| Number | Reciprocal | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | $\frac{1}{3}$ | $3 \times \frac{1}{3} = 1$ |
| $\frac{1}{2}$ | 2 | $\frac{1}{2} \times 2 = 1$ |
| $\frac{3}{4}$ | $\frac{4}{3}$ | $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{4}{3} = 1$ |
| $1\frac{3}{8} = \frac{11}{8}$ | $\frac{8}{11}$ | $\frac{11}{8} \times \frac{8}{11} = 1$ |

## "Of" means multiply

$\frac{7}{10}$ of $8\frac{1}{3}$ m is $\frac{7}{10} \times \frac{25}{3} = \frac{175}{30} = 5\frac{5}{6}$ m.

## The mistake to avoid

When dividing, only the **second** fraction is flipped. This working is wrong:

$$3\tfrac{2}{3} \div 1\tfrac{1}{11} = \tfrac{11}{3} \div \tfrac{12}{11} \;\;\xrightarrow{\ \text{wrong!}\ }\;\; \tfrac{3}{11} \times \tfrac{11}{12}$$

The first fraction was flipped as well. Correctly:
$\frac{11}{3} \times \frac{11}{12} = \frac{121}{36} = 3\frac{13}{36}$.

> **Sense check.** Dividing by a mixed number (bigger than 1) must give a **smaller** answer.
> Dividing by a proper fraction (less than 1) must give a **bigger** one.
